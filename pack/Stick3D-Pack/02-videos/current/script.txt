Three wrecked T-34s roll into a Soviet yard. The mechanic strips them apart and shuffles the parts like a deck of cards. Over the fence, a German officer polishes his one perfect Tiger. He spends months on that single tank. The Soviet spends a week, bolts everything back together, and all three tanks drive away. Then, in nineteen forty two, the Tiger finally rolls out. Its gun can hit a T-34 from a mile away, and their shells just bounce off. The officer paints a ring on the barrel for every kill. But autumn brings rain, and the roads turn to mud. Soviet tanks crawl through it. The Tiger sinks to its belly. Then winter comes. Mud freezes between its wheels overnight, and one morning, the gearbox cracks. Fixing a Tiger is like fixing a pocket watch. It can take a month. The Soviet just drops a broken engine and grabs another. Germany built about thirteen hundred Tigers. The Soviets built over fifty thousand T-34s. Every one was the same underneath, so the whole front became a spare parts shop. A wheel from this one, a gun from that one, and a hammer. Perfect tanks win battles. Tanks you can fix win wars.
